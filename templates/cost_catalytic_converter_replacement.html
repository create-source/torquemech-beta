Catalytic Converter Replacement Cost

Catalytic converter replacement cost usually depends on exhaust layout, converter location, emissions requirements, and whether upstream drivability faults have already been diagnosed. This page gives you a grounded baseline before you move into the estimator.

Estimate Catalytic Converter Replacement

Average Cost

$1,100 to $3,500+

Typical total depends on whether the vehicle uses a bolt-on unit, manifold-style converter, or higher-cost direct-fit emissions hardware.

Labor Time

1.5 to 4.0 hours

Labor varies with rust, sensor removal, exhaust access, and whether the converter is integrated into a manifold assembly.

Parts Cost

$700 to $2,800+

Converter pricing changes quickly based on emissions certification, precious-metal content, and direct-fit versus universal design.

Common Symptoms

  • Check engine light with catalyst-efficiency codes
  • Failed emissions inspection
  • Rattling from a damaged converter substrate
  • Reduced power if the converter is restricted
  • Sulfur or overheated exhaust smell after prolonged running issues

What Affects Cost

  • Federal versus California or CARB-compliant parts
  • Converter location and exhaust disassembly time
  • Need for oxygen sensors, gaskets, or hardware
  • Rust, seized fasteners, or fabrication work
  • Whether upstream fuel or misfire issues need correction first

Service Overview

Converter quotes are only solid after the failure is confirmed. Good shops verify catalyst efficiency, check fuel-trim and misfire history, and make sure a bad sensor or engine-running issue is not the real reason the converter code set in the first place.

  • Confirm catalyst-efficiency failure with scan data and exhaust testing
  • Inspect for upstream misfire, fuel-control, or oil-burning problems
  • Remove the failed converter and any affected sensors or hardware
  • Install the replacement converter with new seals as needed
  • Verify code behavior and exhaust operation after the repair
