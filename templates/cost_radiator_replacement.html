Radiator Replacement Cost

Radiator replacement cost usually depends on cooling-system access, the amount of front-end teardown required, and whether the job needs hoses, fans, or coolant service at the same time. This page gives a clean baseline before you move into the estimator.

Estimate Radiator Replacement

Estimated Cost

$350 to $1,000+

Typical total depends on vehicle design, labor access, and whether related cooling-system parts are replaced at the same time.

Labor Time

1.5 to 3.5 hours

Straightforward jobs move quickly, while tighter radiator supports, fan assemblies, and trim removal can add time.

Parts Cost

$150 to $600+

Price varies based on radiator size, material, integrated cooler design, and aftermarket versus OE quality.

Replacement Signs

  • Coolant leaking under the front of the vehicle
  • Engine overheating or running hotter than normal
  • Visible radiator cracks, corrosion, or fin damage
  • Low coolant level with no obvious hose failure
  • Cooling fan running excessively after warm-up

What Affects Cost

  • Radiator location and front-end access
  • Vehicle make, model, and engine size
  • Whether hoses, clamps, or fan parts also need service
  • Coolant type and refill or bleed procedure
  • Part type: aftermarket, premium aftermarket, or OE

Service Overview

Radiator work is more than just swapping the core. A careful repair includes leak confirmation, safe coolant handling, and a proper refill and bleed so the engine does not trap air and overheat again.

  • Pressure-test the cooling system and confirm the radiator is the leak source
  • Drain coolant and remove covers, fans, or hoses as needed
  • Replace the radiator and inspect mounting points and hose connections
  • Refill with the correct coolant and bleed trapped air
  • Verify operating temperature and check for leaks after warm-up
