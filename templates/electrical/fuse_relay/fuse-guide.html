Fuse Guide

Learn what automotive fuses do, how to test them, and how to identify common fuse-related problems.

Overview

A fuse protects a circuit by opening when current exceeds a safe limit. If a fuse blows, it usually means the circuit had an overload or a short.

Why fuses matter

  • Protect wiring from overheating
  • Protect modules and components from excessive current
  • Help isolate circuit faults
  • Prevent bigger electrical damage

Common signs of a blown fuse

  • A single accessory suddenly stops working
  • No power at a light, horn, radio, or outlet
  • Circuit works again after replacing fuse, then blows again
  • Visible broken fuse element

Basic testing method

  • Turn the key or circuit on if needed
  • Use a test light or multimeter
  • Check for power on both test points of the fuse
  • If only one side has power, the fuse is blown
  • If neither side has power, trace the feed side of the circuit

Important tip

Do not keep replacing a blown fuse without finding the cause. Repeated fuse failure usually points to a shorted wire, failed component, or overloaded circuit.
